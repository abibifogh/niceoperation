The problem

Every system answers its own question well. Nobody can ask across them.

A hotel usually ends up with one tool per department, and no way to hold two of their numbers at once. That is where the money hides.

The wage bill went up

Is the hotel busier, or is it just going up? Attendance knows the hours. It has never seen the takings.

The restaurant took less

Did fewer guests stay, or did fewer of the guests who stayed eat in? Two systems, two screens, a calculator.

The till was short on Tuesday

Who was actually on the premises? The POS records the drawer. It cannot see the staff entrance.

Two arms, one supplier

The kitchen and the restaurant both buy tomatoes. Nothing in the building compares what each one paid.

The suite

Eight systems, each for a job somebody is already doing.

They run independently, on their own addresses, with their own permissions — a housekeeper's PIN opens the bed check and nothing else. A property can take one or all of them. Every one of them is still in development.

How it works

Recorded once, on the floor. Everything else is derived.

The floor records the fact

A punch at the staff entrance, a fridge counted at the shelf, an order sent from a table, a bed marked occupied. Each takes seconds, on a phone or a fixed terminal, from somebody who has no time. Facts are never edited.

Each system holds its own

Every judgement — late, absent, short, low, overdue — is worked out from those facts against the rota, the levels and the rules in setup. Change a rule and every past report is right again, rather than there being two versions of the truth.

Insight asks across all of them

Overnight, every system's numbers are forced into one shape. In the morning there is one screen: a single figure for the group, how each part has moved, and the findings ranked by what they are worth a month.

The approach

Built inside a working operation, not around a demo.

Written where the work happens

Every one of these started as a problem on a real floor: a drawer that would not balance, a fault nobody logged, a file three people were looking for. They are shaped by the shift, not by a feature list.

Usable at the end of a long day

The people using these are not at a desk. They are at a pass, on a corridor, closing a till at one in the morning. Screens are built to be read at a glance and operated with one hand.

Honest about the money

Cash is counted against what the records say, differences have to be explained while somebody still remembers, and anything corrected afterwards leaves a trail with a name on it.

Standing up to a bad connection

Hospitality happens on mobile data and hotel wifi. Work carries on when the signal goes and catches up when it returns, rather than stopping until somebody finds the router.

Questions

Before you ask.

Can I use any of this today?

No. Every system is in development and running only in the property it was built in. If you would like to hear when one opens, write and say which part of your operation is costing you the most time.

Is it one product or eight?

Eight, deliberately. Each runs on its own address with its own database and its own permissions, so no system can take another one down and nobody is given access to more than their job needs. Insight is the ninth thing: the sign-in and the reporting layer that reads across all of them.

Does it handle room bookings?

No, and that matters enough to say twice. There is no property management system among these tools, so every group total Insight produces is understated by the largest line in a hotel's accounts. Insight says so on the same screen as the totals, every time. A dashboard that quietly omitted it would be worse than no dashboard, because it would be believed.

What happens when the internet goes down?

The systems that have to keep working keep working. The point of sale carries on taking orders and syncs when the connection returns; the breakfast count falls back to the device's own storage. Hospitality happens on hotel wifi, and a tool that stops when the signal does is a tool nobody trusts.

Do people need a login for each one?

Once Insight is in place, no. One sign-in there opens every system in the suite — click a system on the hub and you arrive already signed in, as yourself, with whatever that system says you may do. Staff who never touch a computer, like the people the attendance terminal recognises, need no login at all.

Where does the data live?

Each system keeps its own, on its own infrastructure. Insight holds a copy in a warehouse where nothing is a source of truth: every row in it is derived, and every row can be rebuilt by running the loader again.
